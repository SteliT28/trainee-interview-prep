Interview Etiquette

Small details can make a big difference. Choose the type of interview you have and use the guide and checklist to prepare.

What type of interview do you have?

Video interview

Make it easy for the interviewer to focus on you. Test your technology early and create a quiet, tidy and professional setting.

  • Join a few minutes early so you are not rushing when the interview starts.
  • Check your camera, microphone, internet connection and the interview link beforehand.
  • Choose a quiet, well-lit space with a simple, tidy background.
  • Position the camera at a comfortable eye level and look towards it when speaking.
  • Dress as you would for a professional interview and keep your phone on silent.
  • Have your CV and a few useful notes nearby, but avoid reading answers word-for-word.
You do not need perfect technology or perfect confidence — being prepared and calm is what matters.

Phone interview

Without facial expressions and body language, your voice does more of the work. Aim to sound clear, interested and calm.

  • Find a quiet place where you will not be interrupted.
  • Keep your phone charged and check that you have a reliable signal.
  • Have your CV, the job details and a few short notes in front of you.
  • Speak clearly and avoid rushing. A short pause before answering is completely fine.
  • Smile while you speak — it can help your voice sound warmer and more engaged.
  • Do not multitask, eat or let background noise distract you during the call.
You cannot be seen, so focus on letting your voice show your enthusiasm, professionalism and willingness to learn.

Face-to-face interview

First impressions start before the interview begins. Plan your journey, arrive prepared and treat everyone you meet professionally.

  • Plan your route and allow extra time for delays or finding the practice.
  • Aim to arrive around 5–10 minutes early rather than exactly on time.
  • Wear clean, tidy and professional clothing suitable for a dental practice.
  • Greet reception staff politely and remember that your interview starts from the moment you arrive.
  • Make appropriate eye contact, listen carefully and avoid interrupting.
  • Keep your phone away during the interview and thank the interviewer before leaving.
You do not have to look like an experienced dental nurse — show that you are prepared, respectful and ready to learn.

Communication Help

Use these ready-to-adapt scripts when contacting dental practices. Keep your tone natural and professional, and personalise the details before sending or calling.

Phone

Asking if they have a vacancy

Use when you are contacting a practice without seeing a specific advertised vacancy.

“Good morning. My name is [Name]. I’m currently looking for a trainee dental nurse position and I wanted to ask whether your practice has any trainee vacancies at the moment, or whether you might be taking on a trainee in the near future. I’d be very happy to send over my CV and any other information you need. May I ask who would be the best person to send it to?”
Phone

Asking about a current vacancy

Use when you have seen a specific vacancy and want to make a professional enquiry.

“Good morning. My name is [Name]. I’m calling about the trainee dental nurse vacancy I saw advertised. I’m very interested in the position and wanted to ask whether the vacancy is still open. I’m currently looking to begin my dental nursing career and I’d be happy to provide my CV and any other information you require. Could you please let me know the best way to apply?”
Phone

Checking for feedback after an interview

Use a few days after an interview when you have not yet heard back.

“Good morning. My name is [Name]. I interviewed for the trainee dental nurse position on [date], and I’m calling to politely ask whether there has been any update regarding the outcome. I’m still very interested in the position and I would also be grateful for any feedback you’re able to provide. I completely understand if a decision has not yet been made.”
Phone

Following up after sending an application

Useful when you have sent your CV or application and want to check it reached the right person.

“Good morning. My name is [Name]. I recently sent my CV regarding trainee dental nurse opportunities and I’m just calling to check that it reached the appropriate person. I’m very interested in the opportunity to train with your practice. If you need any further information from me, I’d be happy to provide it.”
Email

Asking if they have a vacancy

A short speculative enquiry when there is no advertised vacancy.

Subject: Trainee Dental Nurse Enquiry – [Your Name]\n\nDear [Practice Manager / Name],\n\nI am writing to ask whether your practice currently has, or may have in the near future, an opportunity for a trainee dental nurse. I am very interested in starting my dental nursing career and would be keen to learn and develop within a dental practice.\n\nI would be happy to send my CV and any further information you may require. Please find my contact details below.\n\nKind regards,\n[Full Name]\n[Phone number]\n[Email address]
Email

Presenting yourself and leaving contact details

Use when you want to introduce yourself professionally and make it easy for a practice to contact you.

Subject: Trainee Dental Nurse – [Your Name]\n\nDear [Practice Manager / Name],\n\nMy name is [Full Name], and I am currently looking for an opportunity to begin my training as a dental nurse. I am particularly interested in gaining practical experience in a professional dental environment and developing the skills needed for the role.\n\nI would be very grateful if you could keep my details in mind should a suitable trainee opportunity become available.\n\nYou can contact me on:\nPhone: [Phone number]\nEmail: [Email address]\n\nThank you for your time and consideration.\n\nKind regards,\n[Full Name]
Email

Sending your CV

Use when a practice has asked for your CV or when you are making a professional application.

Subject: CV – Trainee Dental Nurse – [Your Name]\n\nDear [Practice Manager / Name],\n\nThank you for your time. As discussed, please find attached my CV for your consideration for a trainee dental nurse opportunity.\n\nI am very interested in the opportunity to train within your practice and would be happy to provide any additional information or attend an interview if required.\n\nThank you for considering my application.\n\nKind regards,\n[Full Name]\n[Phone number]\n[Email address]
Email

Sending your letter of enrolment

Use when a practice needs confirmation of your dental nursing course or enrolment.

Subject: Letter of Enrolment – [Your Name]\n\nDear [Practice Manager / Name],\n\nAs requested, please find attached my letter of enrolment confirming my place on the [course name] dental nursing programme.\n\nPlease let me know if you require any further information or documentation from me.\n\nThank you for your time.\n\nKind regards,\n[Full Name]\n[Phone number]\n[Email address]

Tip: Replace the bracketed details before sending. Keep the message personalised, concise and professional, and check that your CV or requested document is actually attached before you send the email.
